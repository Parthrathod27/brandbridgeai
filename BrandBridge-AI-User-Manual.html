1What BrandBridge AI does

BrandBridge AI is a collaboration marketplace. Instead of every brand buying its own audience, two compatible brands partner on a shared campaign — splitting the cost while doubling the reach. The platform finds the partner, drafts the outreach, generates the campaign assets, and brings in freelance creatives to produce the work.

The problem it solves

A small brand spends its entire marketing budget acquiring an audience another brand already has. If a coffee roaster and a bakery share the same customer, they are competing for the same ad impressions rather than sharing them. BrandBridge AI makes that introduction, then handles the paperwork and production around it.

Core concepts

ConceptWhat it means
MatchAn AI-scored compatibility rating (0–100%) between two brands, based on audience overlap, industry alignment and shared goals
CollaborationA partnership between two brands. Starts as a request, becomes active once accepted
CampaignThe actual marketing initiative run under a collaboration — with a budget, dates, goals and milestones
ProjectA piece of creative work posted for freelancers to bid on
ProposalA freelancer's bid on a project
HireAn engaged freelancer working on agreed deliverables at an agreed rate
ProductAn item a Product Owner wants brands to promote

The four roles

Your role is chosen once during onboarding and determines your entire dashboard. Each role sees a different sidebar, different statistics and different pages.

RoleYou are…You mainly do
BrandA business with an audience and a marketing budgetFind partner brands, run joint campaigns, hire creatives
Product OwnerSomeone with a product needing exposureList products, find brands to promote them, track performance
FreelancerA designer, editor, copywriter or similarShowcase work, bid on projects, get hired, track earnings

One role per account A role is selected at onboarding and the app routes you to that role's dashboard on every login. Visiting another role's URL redirects you back to your own. To work in a different role you need a separate account.

3Creating an account

The signup flow

Sign up→ Email OTP→ Choose role→ Complete profile→ Dashboard

Step 1 — Sign up

Go to /signup. Enter your name, email and password. The password strength meter updates live as you type. Submitting sends a one-time code to your email address.

Step 2 — Verify the code

You land on /verify-otp. Enter the six-digit code from your inbox. The field auto-advances between boxes and accepts a pasted code. A countdown shows when you may request a new code.

Step 3 and 4

Covered in section 4 — you cannot reach the dashboard until both are done.

Signing in with Google

Press “Continue with Google” on either the login or signup page. Google-authenticated accounts skip OTP verification, since Google has already verified the address. You still choose a role and complete a profile.

Google accounts have no password on the platform, so the change-password section of settings is hidden for them.

Forgotten password

/forgot-password→ Emailed link→ /reset-password→ Sign in

Enter your email at /forgot-password and a reset link is sent. The link carries a single-use token; opening it takes you to /reset-password where you set a new password. Requesting a reset does not reveal whether an account exists, which prevents the form being used to discover registered addresses.

4Onboarding

Choosing your role

At /onboarding/role you pick one of the four roles from section 1. This is the single most important choice in the app — it determines your navigation, your dashboard and which features exist for you at all. Read the four descriptions before choosing.

Completing your profile

At /onboarding/profile you fill in the fields your role requires. The app blocks access to the dashboard until the profile is marked complete, so this is not optional.

What each role is asked for

RoleFields that count toward completeness
BrandCompany name, logo, bio, industry, location, website, target audience, budget range, collaboration preferences, business type — ten fields, each worth 10%
FreelancerProfile photo, bio, location, at least one skill, a category, an hourly rate above zero, and at least one portfolio item — seven checks
Product Owner / HirerCompany name, bio, industry, target audience — four fields, each worth 25%

The completeness widget

Your profile page shows a live percentage bar with the specific fields still missing, phrased as actions — “Upload brand logo”, “Set hourly rate”. Completeness matters beyond tidiness: brand matching quality depends on how much the AI knows about you, and freelancers with incomplete profiles surface lower in the marketplace.

Empty is not the same as zero For freelancers, an hourly rate of 0 counts as unset, not as “free”. Set a real rate or the check stays unfilled.

The complete brand workflow

Complete profile→ Review AI matches→ Send proposal→ Collaboration accepted→ Create campaign→ Hire freelancers→ Track deliverables

6.1  AI brand matching

The matching page scores potential partners from 0–100% using your industry, audience definition and stated collaboration goals — which is exactly why profile completeness matters. Each card shows the score, why the brands fit, and actions to open the full profile, save the match, or start outreach.

Alongside registered brands the page can surface external brand suggestions — companies not yet on the platform that the AI identifies as a good fit, useful for offline outreach.

Sending outreach

Choosing a match and pressing outreach opens the AI-drafted email modal. The draft is generated from both brands' profiles. Always read and edit it before sending — it is a starting point, not a finished email.

6.2  Collaborations

A collaboration is pending until the other brand responds, then accepted or declined. Incoming requests appear here with accept and decline actions; opening one shows the full partner profile and the proposal text.

6.3  Campaigns

Campaigns run under an accepted collaboration — you cannot create one without a partner, which is deliberate, since a campaign is by definition shared. Press New Campaign and provide:

  • Campaign name and the linked collaboration
  • Type — co-branded social post, product bundle, influencer push, cross-promotion, or other
  • Goal — awareness, sales, engagement or reach
  • Start and end dates, and the budget

Campaign status moves through draft, active, paused, completed or cancelled. Opening a campaign shows performance metrics and the cost split between both brands.

6.4  Hiring freelancers

The marketplace lists verified creatives filterable by category, skill, rate and rating. Opening a freelancer shows their bio, portfolio, skills and reviews. Hire opens a form for the agreed rate, the deadline, an optional linked campaign, and the deliverables expected.

Hires progress through pending → active → completed, or cancelled. Under My Hires you review submitted deliverables and leave a review once work is complete.

7.1  Managing products

Add a product with its name, description, category, pricing and images. Status is draft, active or archived — only active products can be attached to campaigns or shown to brands, so remember to activate after drafting.

Opening a product shows an image gallery, an analytics panel, and an AI marketing generator that produces copy, SEO tags or hashtags for that specific product.

7.2  The dashboard customiser

Press Customize in the dashboard toolbar to show or hide individual widgets — KPIs, AI insights, quick actions, recent activity, upcoming meetings, goal progress and the performance chart. The toolbar also opens the notification centre, the task board, saved reports and the document library.

7.3  The campaign wizard

1 · Core details→ 2 · Milestones & goals→ 3 · Budget & ROI

Step one captures the title, description and linked active product. Step two sets milestones — pre-filled with a sensible default sequence (contract signed, content draft approval, campaign live) that you can edit. Step three sets the budget and shows the projected return.

An Ask AI button at any step suggests content and positioning ideas for the campaign you are describing. Creating a campaign also registers a workflow automation that generates tasks and notifies your team when the campaign is approved.

7.4  AI Hub

Five tools sharing one console. Pick a tool, fill the prompt, and the result appears in a copyable panel:

The freelancer workflow

Build portfolio→ Browse projects→ Submit proposal→ Accepted → hired→ Deliver work→ Get paid & reviewed

8.1  Portfolio first

Do this before anything else. Your profile is not complete without at least one portfolio item, and an incomplete profile keeps you out of marketplace results. Each item takes a title, description, media and a category.

8.2  Projects and proposals

Browse Projects lists work with status open. Each shows the budget — fixed or hourly — the scope and the skills wanted. Submitting a proposal records your bid amount, timeline and pitch.

Proposals are pending, then accepted or rejected. You may withdraw your own proposal while it is still pending. An accepted proposal becomes a hire.

8.3  Hire requests and delivery

A brand or hirer can also approach you directly. Pending hire requests appear on your dashboard with the offered rate, deadline and deliverables — accept or decline from there. Once active, upload deliverables against the hire; the client reviews them and can then leave a rating.

8.4  Earnings

Totals, completed job count and per-hire payment status. Figures derive from your hire records, so they update as hires move to completed.

The hirer workflow

Post a project→ Receive proposals→ Accept one→ Hire created→ Review deliverables→ Complete & rate

Two routes to a hire

Post and wait — create a project with a title, scope, budget type and amount, and required skills. Freelancers bid; you compare proposals and accept one.

Search and approach — browse the marketplace, open a freelancer, and hire them directly with an agreed rate and deliverables. Save interesting freelancers for later; they appear on your dashboard.

Projects move through open → in_progress → completed, or closed if you stop taking bids.

Usage limits

To keep costs predictable, AI requests are capped per user:

LimitAllowanceApplies to
Per individual feature12 per minuteEach AI endpoint separately
Across all AI features30 per minuteEverything combined

Exceeding a limit returns a message telling you how many seconds to wait. Nothing is lost — retry after the countdown.

When AI output looks generic

Two causes. Either GEMINI_API_KEY is not configured, so the route is serving its built-in fallback text; or your profile is thin, leaving the model little to work with. Completing your profile measurably improves match and proposal quality.

Always review AI output before it leaves the platform Outreach emails, proposals and campaign copy are drafts. They can contain plausible but incorrect details about either brand. Read them properly before sending.

12Settings and security

Settings lives at /dashboard/<your-role>/settings and has four sections.

Account

Update your display name and email address. Changing an email requires verifying the new address before it takes effect — until then the old address stays active and the pending change is shown.

Password

Requires your current password plus the new one. Hidden entirely for Google-authenticated accounts, which have no platform password.

Security

Two-factor authentication

Enable 2FA→ Scan QR code→ Enter 6-digit code→ Active

The app generates a QR code for any authenticator app — Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password. Scan it, then confirm with a generated code. 2FA is only active once that confirmation succeeds, which prevents locking yourself out with a mis-scanned code.

Active sessions

Lists every device signed into your account with its browser, operating system and last-active time. Revoke any session you do not recognise; that device is signed out immediately.

Deleting your account

Permanent. You must type your email address to confirm, and password-based accounts must also supply their password. Do this only if you are certain — there is no undo.

Notifications

Notification preferences are stored per account and control which platform events reach you.

Workflow automations can fire on: campaign approved, collaboration accepted, task completed, product activated, project approved, hire accepted, payment released, project completed, and promotion accepted. Available actions are: create a task, notify the team, generate a report, and send an email.

14Troubleshooting

SymptomCause and fix
Redirected to profile completion repeatedlyA required field is still empty. The completeness widget names exactly which one
Cannot create a campaignCampaigns require an accepted collaboration. A pending one is not enough
Product not selectable in a campaignOnly active products can be linked. Change it from draft
“Too many AI requests”You hit the rate limit. Wait the number of seconds shown
AI output feels genericEither GEMINI_API_KEY is unset and fallback text is being served, or your profile is too sparse
Not appearing in the freelancer marketplaceYour profile is incomplete — most often a missing portfolio item or an hourly rate of zero
Signed out unexpectedlyThe session was revoked from Settings → Security, or the password changed on another device
Another role's URL bounces backExpected. Accounts are locked to one role
Images not loading after uploadCloudinary credentials are probably missing, so uploads fall back to inline base64 — functional, but heavy on large files
OTP email never arrivesCheck spam, then verify GMAIL_USER and GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD. A Gmail app password is required, not the account password
“This section could not be loaded”A request failed. Press Retry — your data is unaffected

Getting help

When an error screen shows a reference code, quote it — it matches an entry in the server logs and identifies the exact failure.

Keyboard shortcuts Ctrl+K opens global search from anywhere in the dashboard. Esc closes any open modal.
